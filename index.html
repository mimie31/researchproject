📖 The Bioscience Scenario

In intensive urban aquaponics production (e.g., commercial Red Tilapia strain), fish stock growth is non-linear and conforms to a sigmoidal (S-shaped) logistic curve. Understanding the instantaneous rate of change—the First Derivative \( f'(x) \)—is critical for precision aquaculture ecosystem design.

The derivative represents the daily average weight gain (grams/day). In stock management, identifying when the growth rate peaks is vital. Once the derivative peaks and begins to decline, the fish's feed conversion efficiency drops. Keeping tilapia past this point increases operational expenses rapidly, as feed represents nearly 70% of total production costs in Malaysian aquaculture.

📊 Mathematical Growth Model

Cumulative Growth \( f(x) \):
$$f(x) = \frac{W_{target}}{1 + \beta \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x}}$$
Derivative \( f'(x) \) (Daily Growth Rate):
$$f'(x) = \frac{W_{target} \cdot \beta \cdot (k \cdot \eta) \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x}}{(1 + \beta \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x})^2}$$
\( x \): Day of Growth cycle (0 to 120)
\( W_{target} \): Target asymptotic weight (g)
\( \beta \): Initial scaling parameter (fixed 90)
\( k \): Biological growth rate coefficient
\( \eta \): Biofilter Recirculation & Comfort Index (0.7 to 1.15)
